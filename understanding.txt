ISO 55001 is an international standard that provides a step-by-step framework for organizations to manage their assets effectively. 
It helps companies get the most value out of their equipment, buildings, or finances, while balancing costs, risks, 
performance, and sustainability throughout their entire lifespan.

The Whole Lifecycle: It isn't just about maintaining things when they break. 
It governs an asset from its birth (planning and purchasing) to its operation, maintenance, and eventual disposal.

Why do companies use it?
Organizations adopt this standard to:Reduce operating costs by eliminating unnecessary expenses and reactive repairs.
Maximize the return on investment from everything they own.
Minimize risks like unexpected downtime or safety hazards


ISO 55001 - International Standard for Asset Management Systems
- ISO 55001 is a framework that helps organizations manage their assets throughout their entire lifecycle to maximize value, 
reduce risk, and control costs.
- It helps organizations manage assets efficiently from acquisition to disposal while balancing cost, risk, performance, and compliance.
Example:
- A factory doesn't only repair a machine when it breaks. 
It plans maintenance, tracks risk, manages lifecycle cost, and decides when replacement is better than repair.


RCM - Reliability Centered Maintenance
- A methodology used to determine the most effective maintenance strategy for an asset.
- RCM helps decide whether an asset should be maintained through preventive maintenance, 
predictive maintenance, condition monitoring, or run-to-failure.
Example:
- A critical production pump may require vibration monitoring, while a light bulb can simply be replaced when it fails.


MTBF - Mean Time Between Failures
Calculated like
- MTBF = Total Operating Time ÷ Number of Failures

- Measures how long an asset typically operates before failing.
- MTBF tells us the reliability of an asset. Higher MTBF means the asset fails less frequently.
Example:
- Machine runs 1000 hours and fails 5 times.
- MTBF = 1000 ÷ 5 = 200 hours
Meaning: On average the machine fails every 200 hours.


MTTR - Mean Time To Repair
Calculated like
- MTTR = Total Repair Time ÷ Number of Repairs

- Measures how quickly maintenance teams restore equipment after a failure.
- MTTR indicates maintainability. Lower MTTR means assets are repaired faster.
Example:
- 5 breakdowns required 20 hours of repair.
- MTTR = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 hours
Meaning: It takes 4 hours on average to repair the equipment.


Availability
Calculated Like
- Availability = MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR)

- Shows how often an asset is available for production.
Example:
- MTBF = 200 hrs, MTTR = 4 hrs
- Availability = 200 / (200+4) = 98%
Meaning: The asset is available for production 98% of the time.


RCA - Root Cause Analysis
- A structured process used to find the actual cause of a problem instead of only fixing symptoms.
- RCA identifies why a failure occurred and helps prevent it from happening again.
Example:
Pump stopped working
Problem: Motor burned
Why?: Bearing failed
Why?: Lubrication was missing
Root Cause: Lubrication procedure not followed


FMEA - Failure Modes and Effects Analysis
- A proactive method used to identify possible failures before they happen.
- FMEA evaluates potential failure modes, their impact, and the actions needed to reduce risk.
Example:
| Component | Failure Mode | Effect          |
| --------- | ------------ | --------------- |
| Bearing   | Wear         | Pump failure    |
| Motor     | Overheating  | Production loss |


PM - Preventive Maintenance
- Maintenance performed at planned intervals before failure occurs.
- PM is scheduled maintenance aimed at preventing breakdowns.
Example:
- Changing oil every 3 months.


PdM - Predictive Maintenance
- Maintenance performed based on asset condition and data analysis.
- Predictive Maintenance uses sensors and analytics to predict failures before they happen.
Example
- Replacing a bearing after vibration readings indicate deterioration.


CBM - Condition-Based Maintenance
- Maintenance triggered when asset condition reaches a threshold.
- CBM performs maintenance based on actual equipment condition instead of fixed schedules.
Example:
Replace a bearing only when vibration exceeds a predefined limit.


SMRP - Society for Maintenance & Reliability Professionals
- A globally recognized body that publishes maintenance and reliability best practices.
- SMRP provides standards and best practices for maintenance planning, reliability, work management, and performance measurement.


RUL - Remaining Useful Life
- Estimated amount of useful operating time left before an asset is likely to fail.
- RUL predicts how much life remains in an asset before replacement or repair is required.
Example:
- A bearing is expected to last another 6 months based on sensor data.


LOTO - Lockout / Tagout
- A safety procedure that ensures equipment is completely isolated before maintenance work starts.
- LOTO prevents accidental startup of equipment during maintenance activities.
Example:
- Locking electrical power before servicing a motor.


PTW - Permit To Work
- A formal authorization process required before performing hazardous work.
- PTW ensures all safety precautions are reviewed and approved before work begins.
Example:
- Hot work permit for welding.


ST - Shutdown & Turnaround (S&T)
- A planned period when equipment or an entire plant is taken offline for major maintenance and upgrades.
- Shutdown and Turnaround management coordinates large-scale maintenance activities to minimize downtime and risk.
Example:
- Annual refinery shutdown for inspections and equipment replacement.
